Scorecard Tiers

Critical / Major / Minor · reject-on-sight rules

Critical - Reject / Re-plan

Geographic miss · serial OAR over hard tolerance · hotspot in critical organ · undeliverable

Major - Correct or justify

Parallel-OAR mean over constraint · V95 borderline · avoidable beam through OAR

Minor - Comment only

Slightly high MU · minor inhomogeneity · non-ideal but workable isocentre

Plan verdict categories

Bad · Acceptable · Trade-off · Good - the skill is triage

Reject on sight if… (Slide 26)
①Cold dose reaching into the CTV - not just the PTV margin
②A critical serial OAR exceeds its hard tolerance (cord, brainstem, chiasm)
③Plan is unsafe or undeliverable as drawn
The gut-check (Slide 4)“Would I confidently treat my own family member with this plan?” If the answer isn't a clear yes, name exactly what would have to change.

The Four Central Questions (Slide 3)

  • Does it treat the target? Adequate dose, no geographic miss, no cold CTV
  • Does it spare normal tissue? Every OAR within tolerance for that organ type
  • Is it technically sound? Sensible isocentre, beam angles, weighting and shaping
  • Is it deliverable and robust? Achievable on machine; stable to setup variation

Coverage Metrics - Block 1

  • PTV D95 >95% - standard coverage criterion
  • CTV D98 >98% - the clinical target must be fully covered
  • HI - (D2%−D98%)/D50% <0.1 good; wedges / FiF to improve
  • CI - PIV/TV; ideal 1.0–1.2; higher in 3DCRT vs IMRT is expected
  • D2% <107% - near-max dose; hotspot criterion (ICRU 83)

OAR Constraint Logic - Block 2

  • Serial (cord, brainstem, chiasm): Dmax governs - one failed unit = organ failure
  • Parallel (lung, parotid, kidney): Dmean or Vx governs - redundant sub-units
  • QUANTEC 2010 - evidence-based; assumes ~2 Gy/fr conventional 3DCRT
  • Context - re-irradiation, concurrent chemo, comorbidities all tighten limits
  • PRV - evaluate dose to PRV for serial OARs, not bare organ

Technical Red Flags - Block 3

  • Isocentre in lung / air / density interface - dosimetrically unstable
  • Beam transiting through critical OAR - re-angle if workable alternative exists
  • Hotspot outside PTV - in skin, bowel, or OAR: always a problem
  • MLC clipped tight to PTV - cold rim guaranteed; leave penumbra margin
  • No wedge on sloping contour / no flash - cold strip / undercoverage

Good Plan (FiF) - Key Features

  • Isocentre commonly placed in lung - acceptable for breast tangents; field sizes balanced
  • Fields do not cross midline - contralateral breast and lung fully spared
  • Flash ≥2 cm beyond breast tissue edge - superficial coverage, breathing and set-up margin
  • Normalisation point in stable representative breast tissue; PTV D95 >95%
  • CTV D98 >98% - superficial CTV fully covered (bolus or flash)
  • D2% <110% - hotspot inside breast tissue only, not in skin or OAR
  • FiF technique - selective sub-segments reduce hotspots without creating cold areas; HI <0.1
  • Heart mean <2 Gy; ipsilateral lung V20 <20%

Bad → Acceptable → Good - Whole-Breast Tangents

BadNo wedges or FiF; isocentre too deep (into lung & heart); fields cross midline; no flash; normalisation point in lung; hotspot >115%; heart over tolerance.
AcceptableWedged; sensible isocentre; stays in midline (contralateral spared); ≥2 cm flash; normalisation in breast tissue; hotspot <115%; heart & lung within tolerance.
GoodField-in-field selectively removes hotspots; hotspot <110%; HI <0.1 - same coverage and sparing as the acceptable plan, but more homogeneous.
TeachingThe three plans differ mainly in dose shaping: none → wedges → FiF. Coverage and OAR sparing converge; homogeneity is what separates acceptable from good.

  • Cord block on lateral fields; PRV Dmax <45 Gy; dose at block edge documented
  • Electron/photon posterior neck match - at skin surface, coplanar, no cold strip, dose summation documented
  • PTV D95 >95%; GTV D98 >98%; all nodal levels II–V covered
  • D2% <107%; hotspot inside PTV only
  • At least one parotid mean <26 Gy; contralateral sparing documented
  • Mandible Dmax <70 Gy; uninvolved larynx shielded where possible

4-Field (Acceptable) vs 3-Field (Good) - Parotid Tumour

Parotid4-field plan: contralateral parotid (L) mean ≈3.30 Gy vs 1.14 Gy in the 3-field plan - a clinically significant difference for long-term xerostomia and QoL.
CauseThe 4-field arrangement routes beams through the contralateral side - the direct cause of the elevated parotid dose; removing those fields resolves it.
Serial OARSpinal cord max 19.9 Gy (4-field) vs 17.8 Gy (3-field); brainstem max 18.2 vs 16.1 Gy - serial-OAR improvement is a secondary benefit of fewer cross-fields.
TeachingFewer fields ≠ worse plan. Here, removing the fields through the contralateral side is the single most effective intervention for xerostomia prevention.
